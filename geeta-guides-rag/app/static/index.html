ॐchar-GPT inspector

A character-level transformer, written from scratch and trained on 124,135 characters of the Bhagavad Gita. This page runs that model locally and shows what it is doing — the next-character distribution, and what each attention head is looking at.

1 · One forward pass

Type anything. Every keystroke runs a full forward pass and shows the model's prediction for the next character. This distribution is the model's entire output — generation is just sampling from it, appending, and running again.

Next character — top 12

Measurements

Entropy is how undecided the model is, in bits. log₂(79) ≈ 6.30 bits means "no idea"; near 0 means near-certainty. Watch it collapse after a space, when only a few letters can plausibly start a word.

2 · What the attention heads are looking at

Shading shows how much attention the final position paid to every earlier character when predicting the next one. Four layers, four heads each — sixteen different views of the same text. Notice that nothing ever attends to its right: that is the causal mask you wrote.

3 · Generate

One character per forward pass, streamed at the speed it is produced. The text will have the shape of Arnold's blank verse — capitalised line starts, plausible line lengths, pronounceable non-words — and will mean nothing. That is the correct output for a 3.26M-parameter character model.

What this is. A demo of the foundations, running on your
  machine. No API calls, no network.

  
What it is not. Not a question-answering system, and not
  usable as one. Its context window is 256 characters — one verse plus a
  short question fills it entirely, leaving no room to answer in. Retrieval over
  the 700-verse corpus is a separate system; this is the thing that explains why
  that system needs embeddings.
